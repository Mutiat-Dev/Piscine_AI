# First prompt

    "How did Boravia gain their independence"

# Output 

    Boravia had always been a land of mountains and mist, its valleys rich with iron and its rivers strong enough to power a continent. For decades, however, it lived under the distant rule of an empire that valued its resources more than its people. Decisions about Boravia’s schools, trade, and even language were made in faraway halls by leaders who had never walked its rugged cliffs.

    The spark of independence began not with war, but with words. University students in the capital, Velmora, started hosting underground debates about self-determination. Farmers joined them. Miners joined them. Soon, the movement had a name: *The Dawn Assembly*. They demanded fair taxation, local governance, and recognition of Boravian culture.

    When peaceful petitions were ignored, nationwide strikes followed. Railways stopped. Mines fell silent. Markets closed. What surprised the empire most was the unity—northern highlanders and southern riverfolk standing side by side. Even members of the imperial-appointed council quietly pledged support.

    Tensions rose when imperial troops were deployed to “restore order.” But instead of violence, Boravians organized mass candlelight marches. Images of unarmed citizens filling the streets spread beyond Boravia’s borders, drawing international attention and diplomatic pressure on the empire.

    Facing economic strain, global scrutiny, and an ungovernable population, the empire agreed to negotiations. After months of tense talks, a referendum was held under international supervision. The result was overwhelming: Boravians voted for independence.

    On the day the new flag was raised above Velmora’s central square, church bells rang from the mountains to the coast. Independence had not come easily, but it had come through unity, resilience, and the refusal to let their identity fade.

    Boravia was no longer a province. It was a nation—built not just on land and resources, but on the shared will of its people.

# Second prompt 

    "How did Nigeria gain her indepedence?"

# Output

    Nigeria gained her independence through **constitutional negotiations, political activism, and gradual self-governance**, rather than armed struggle.

During British colonial rule (late 19th century to 1960), Nigerian nationalists began demanding greater political representation. Early leaders such as **Herbert Macaulay** laid the foundation for organized resistance. Later, figures like **Nnamdi Azikiwe**, **Obafemi Awolowo**, and **Ahmadu Bello** pushed strongly for self-government through political parties and constitutional reforms.

After World War II, Britain introduced constitutional changes (such as the Richards, Macpherson, and Lyttleton Constitutions) that gradually increased Nigerian participation in governance. By 1957, the Eastern and Western Regions had achieved internal self-government, and the Northern Region followed in 1959.

In 1958–1959, constitutional conferences in London finalized the terms for independence. Following national elections in 1959, **Abubakar Tafawa Balewa** became Prime Minister.

On **October 1, 1960**, Nigeria officially gained independence from Britain. Tafawa Balewa delivered the independence speech, and Nigeria became a sovereign nation within the Commonwealth, with **Nnamdi Azikiwe** serving as Governor-General (and later becoming Nigeria’s first President in 1963 when Nigeria became a republic).

In summary, Nigeria achieved independence through **political negotiation, constitutional development, and nationalist movements**, rather than violent revolution.
